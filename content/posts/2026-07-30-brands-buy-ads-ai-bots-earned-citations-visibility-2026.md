Time has started selling ads to AI bots, with Ally Bank and the Project Management Institute among the first buyers. The move creates a paid layer inside the content AI crawlers ingest — but buying placement in front of a machine is not the same as being cited in its answer. Earned corroboration still decides who the model recommends.

## What Time actually built

Time is converting its webpages into stripped-down markdown versions that AI systems read more easily, then inserting sponsored FAQ blocks into those machine-facing pages. The publisher is running the product with ad tech firm Mobian, [as Digiday first reported](https://digiday.com/media/time-has-started-serving-ads-to-ai-bots/). Mobian builds an ad in FAQ format from a brand brief, routes it through human approval, and then measures results by putting the FAQ questions to AI search engines and scoring how visible and favorable the brand looks in the responses.

One ad runs per markdown page, and Time charges a premium for it. Ally Bank and the Project Management Institute bought the first inventory. This is not an ad a person sees on time.com — it is content engineered to sit in front of ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini as they crawl.

## Why publishers are selling shelf space to machines

The economics are straightforward. Time now sees more AI crawler requests than most of the roughly 7,000 publishers in TollBit's network, and on most days bot traffic exceeds human traffic. As AI answers absorb the clicks that once flowed to publisher sites, that machine attention becomes the inventory left to sell.

"This is a growing traffic source, and therefore a growing source of inventory that we believe has huge value," Time COO Mark Howard told Digiday. Time has been building toward this for months, having [pitched GEO insights as a brand offering earlier in 2026](https://digiday.com/media/time-pitches-geo-insights-into-a-new-brand-offering/) and, alongside Axios, [turned its AI prominence into an advertising pitch](https://pressgazette.co.uk/marketing/time-and-axios-turn-ai-prominence-into-advertising-revenue/). The platforms are moving in parallel: OpenAI has [switched on cost-per-action ads inside ChatGPT](https://digiday.com/marketing/openai-turns-on-cost-per-action-ads-inside-chatgpt/) and has [reached the coupon stage of standing up an ad business](https://digiday.com/marketing/openais-chatgpt-reaches-the-coupon-stage-of-building-an-ad-business/).

## Paid reach is not an earned citation

Mobian CEO Jonah Goodhart framed the appeal bluntly: "Maybe it's more important to influence the agent than even the human. When you influence ChatGPT, you're influencing potentially all of ChatGPT." That is the pitch, and it contains the trap.

Getting a sponsored FAQ into a page a crawler reads guarantees exposure to the machine. It does not guarantee the machine repeats you in an unprompted answer. An AI engine weighs a labeled, single-source sponsored block very differently from a claim corroborated across many independent outlets. Paid bot placement buys presence in the input. A citation is an output the model chooses to produce — and it chooses based on trust signals no single advertiser controls.

## What the data says drives AI recommendations

The strongest predictor of whether an AI engine names a brand is not owned or paid content — it is who independently writes about that brand. Analysis of AI citation behavior shows engines cite third-party earned sources at materially higher rates than brand-controlled pages, a pattern [Machine Relations research on earned-versus-owned citation rates](https://machinerelations.ai/research/earned-vs-owned-ai-citation-rates-2026) tracks directly.

This is the substance behind [earned authority](https://machinerelations.ai/glossary/earned-authority): placements in outlets the models already treat as credible. AuthorityTech's [publication intelligence](https://authoritytech.io/publications) maps which publications AI engines actually cite across verticals, and the concentration is real — a handful of trusted sources drive a disproportionate share of the answers. A sponsored FAQ on one publisher's markdown page enters that competition as a single, disclosed data point, not as the distributed corroboration that moves [AI visibility](https://machinerelations.ai/glossary/ai-visibility).

## Where advertising to bots helps — and where it stalls

Paid bot placement is a real tactic with a narrow job. Treated as awareness against a machine audience, it can seed structured brand facts. Treated as a shortcut to citation, it will disappoint.

| Objective | Paid ads to AI bots | Earned authority |
|---|---|---|
| Put brand facts in front of crawlers | Effective | Effective |
| Get named in an unprompted AI answer | Weak | Strong |
| Build cross-source corroboration | None | Core function |
| Survive a model or ranking update | Fragile | Durable |
| Influence favorability at scale | Limited | Compounding |

The Ally Bank and Project Management Institute buys will likely register in Mobian's visibility scores because those scores measure the FAQ questions the ad was built around. Whether the brands earn mentions in the broader universe of buyer questions is a separate test — and one that paid inventory alone does not pass.

## The Machine Relations read: influence the model on its terms

Goodhart is right that influencing the machine matters. The discipline for doing it is [Machine Relations](https://machinerelations.ai/glossary/machine-relations), the practice [Jaxon Parrott named in 2024](https://jaxonparrott.com/blog/when-ai-stops-being-theoretical) to describe how brands become legible, retrievable, and credible to AI discovery systems like ChatGPT, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews.

Within that frame, Time's product is one input into a system that rewards distributed trust. A brand that only buys sponsored FAQs is optimizing a single node. A brand that earns coverage across many independent outlets, maintains a consistent machine-readable identity, and structures its content for extraction is shaping the corroboration the models weigh. Paid placement can support that work. It cannot substitute for it.

## What CMOs should do with "advertise to the AI" budgets

Treat inventory like Time's as a measurement and awareness line, not a citation line. Buy it to test how a machine reads your brand facts and to seed structured messaging, then judge it on machine-side visibility scores rather than expecting durable answer share.

Fund earned authority separately and weight it heavier. Track where AI engines already cite your category, target the outlets that carry disproportionate citation weight, and build the entity clarity that lets models resolve who you are. When paid bot reach and earned corroboration run together — with earned as the foundation — the paid layer stops being a gimmick and starts being a controllable input. ## FAQ

### Does buying ads on AI bots get my brand cited in ChatGPT?

Not directly. Products like Time's place sponsored content in the pages crawlers read, which guarantees exposure but not citation. AI engines decide what to repeat based on trust and corroboration across independent sources, so paid placement is an input, not an outcome.

### How is advertising to AI bots different from earned media?

Paid bot placement is a single, labeled, brand-controlled block on one publisher's machine-facing page. Earned media is independent coverage across many outlets that AI engines already trust. Citation behavior favors distributed earned corroboration over any single sponsored source.

### Is Time's AI bot ad product worth buying?

As an awareness and measurement tool, potentially. It can seed structured brand facts in front of ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini and score how a machine reads them. As a standalone path to durable AI answer share, it is weak, because one sponsored FAQ cannot build cross-source trust.

### What actually moves AI brand visibility?

Earned authority in outlets AI engines already cite, a consistent machine-readable identity, and content structured for extraction. Machine Relations research shows engines cite independent third-party sources well above brand-owned pages, making earned corroboration the primary driver of AI recommendations.
